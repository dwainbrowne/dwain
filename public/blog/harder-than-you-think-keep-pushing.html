I once priced a car at three websites, and I never got there.

In February 2023 I turned on a webcam and recorded a video with no plan, no filter and no edits. I'd fallen back into the start-stop pattern on my YouTube channel, and I wanted to talk about something I'd carried for more than 20 years: how badly I underestimated the work.

The business case

Whatever you think stands between you and your goal is further away than it looks.

Founders say "just" a lot. Just two more sales. Just one more investor. Just an extra thousand dollars. In my own journey, every one of those turned out to be a lot harder than I pictured.

That gap costs more than time. It lands on your family, your friends and your relationships. So the honest question is how much you want the goal, and what you're willing to pay for it.

Three websites to buy a car

When I finished school, I went to a car dealership with my dad. Back then I wanted a Mitsubishi Evolution. It was a second-hand car, priced in Eastern Caribbean dollars, and it wasn't an expensive car. For me, at that age, it was a lot of money.

The salesman gave us the price, and my dad started weighing it up. I did my own maths. I told him, okay, that's about three websites. I can do that.

I was so confident. In my head the car cost three websites, and I'd have them done fast.

Years later, still not there

I never got to three websites. To be clear, I've built tons of websites since then. The websites were never the problem.

The problem was that I thought it was this simple, and it turned out to be this hard. "Three websites" became my shorthand for that naive picture of how much effort a goal takes.

I never bought the car either. I wouldn't buy it today, except maybe as a racing car.

It's further than you think

You start out so sure. If I just hit this goal, if I just get over this hill, if I just had an extra thousand dollars, I'll get there. Wherever "there" is.

When I recorded that video, I'd been at it for 22 years. One thing I could say without a doubt: whatever that "just" is, it's way further than you think.

How much do you want it?

Around that time I'd been talking with a friend I met in one of my early startup accelerators. Their life had gotten more complicated too: kids, relationship challenges, starting and stopping more than one business.

That's what happens on the way. Extra weight gets dumped on you, and you have to push through it to reach the top of the mountain. I've always been very driven by the things I'm working on, and nobody could get me off that path. So for me the real question became the cost. What does it cost your family, your friendships, your relationship, your kids if you have them?

There's no right or wrong answer. All I can tell you is that it's not three websites. It's a lot more websites.

Maybe your brain has to shortcut it

There's a kinder way to look at it. Maybe if you knew the real effort up front, you wouldn't start. So maybe your brain has to call it three websites.

Your daily commute shows the same trick. You do it so often that your brain stops paying attention, and some days you get to work and realize you don't remember how you got there. Maybe "three websites" is the same kind of shortcut. It hides how much effort the goal will take so you'll get over the first hurdle.

If that's true, my brain has done a fabulous job of shortcutting everything.

Plan for the long haul

The takeaway I gave was simple. Whatever effort or timeline you expect for your goal, whether that's building a company, becoming a programmer or automating your business, it's way further. It's not even double or triple.

So be ready for the long haul. There will be sacrifices along the way, and you'll have to keep weighing them and deciding how badly you want that one thing.

What I'd take from it

If you're looking at your own "three websites" right now, assume the road is longer than it looks. Then decide what you will and won't trade to get there, before the extra weight starts landing.
